2 · The core problem

AI agents can’t work accurately from spreadsheets at this size

An AI model reads a limited amount of text at once (its context window). The current Data Index sheet is far larger than that, so agents read some tabs and miss the rest.

Sheet measured 15 Oct 2026: 87 tabs, 276,873 filled cells, ~9.4M characters, at ~4 characters per token (OpenAI). Context windows from secondary comparisons (MorphLLM, AI Toolbox); they vary by model and plan.

Why agents get confused

~21,800tokens per bid; hundreds of bids ≈ 6.5M+
34 tabsare OLD / ARCHIVE copies holding conflicting versions
60 / minSheets API reads per user, so agents page tab by tab

Even when text fits, models miss facts in the middle of long inputs, and accuracy falls as inputs grow (Liu et al., TACL 2024).

The fix: a database with a search index. The agent asks a precise question through a tool and gets back only the matching records, a few thousand tokens instead of millions.

Sheets API quota: Google. File size is not the issue: Sheets allows 20M cells or 100 MB per file (Google).

Replaces the spreadsheet. Documents are read automatically and checked by staff; competitor awards, pricing and proposals are built from verified data; every bid outcome is recorded.

Build3–6 months
Run cost$150–400/mo
Load history$60–400 once

Cons

  • Staff time to verify extracted facts
  • Largest single build stage

Adds a BigQuery warehouse and public market data: Pwin scoring, price-to-win, recompete calendar, market analysis and lead scoring.

5 · Pwin, price-to-win, leads and market analysis

What ships first, and what waits for data

Scoring can start right away as rules; statistical models become reliable only once enough outcomes are recorded. That’s why outcome capture starts in Option 1.

CapabilityShips first asUpgrades toWhat it needs
PwinWeighted scorecard (incumbency, relationship, price position, past performance, competition), the standard capture practiceCalibrated statistical model, then machine learning~300 bids with recorded outcomes supports only ~3–9 predictors; ML needs ~150–300+ recorded wins
Price-to-winShould-cost plus competitor benchmarksRanges tuned by recorded bid outcomesPublic data: wage determinations, GSA price lists, award values, Texas bid tabulations. Doesn’t depend on L&P’s win history
Lead scoringRules: fit plus trigger signals (expiring contract, new facility, RFP issued)Predictive scoringLogged lead outcomes; Salesforce’s model requires ≥1,000 leads with ≥120 conversions
Market analysisMarket size, competitor share, recompete calendarTrends and forecastsUSAspending, SAM.gov, BLS wage data, Texas ESBD, all free

Sample size: Riley et al., BMJ 2020; Peduzzi et al. 1996 (10 events per variable). Lead scoring: Salesforce Einstein guide. Pwin practice: Shipley, Unanet and Loopio documentation. The predictor counts are our approximation of Riley’s criteria.
